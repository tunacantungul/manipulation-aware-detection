Attention-Guided Manipulation-Aware Detection of AI-Generated and Edited Images for Improved Cross-Dataset Generalization

Department of Computer Engineering, TED University · 2026–2027

About the project

Detectors of AI-generated and AI-edited photographs often report near-perfect accuracy on the dataset they were trained on, yet degrade sharply on images from unseen generators, unseen editing tools, unseen datasets, or after real-world post-processing such as compression and resizing. A likely reason is that detectors learn shortcuts: cues that are correlated with the label in the training data but are not the manipulation itself.

This project investigates whether guiding a detector's attention toward the manipulated regions of an image improves its cross-dataset generalization. We add an attention module to an existing detector and supervise it with manipulation masks, which are available by construction for locally edited (inpainted) photographs. We evaluate the detector under cross-generator, cross-dataset and post-processing shifts, and measure whether its attention stays on the manipulated region when the data distribution changes.

Guiding question Do detectors of AI-generated and edited photographs maintain their in-dataset performance on unseen generators, unseen editing operations, unseen datasets and real-world post-processing, and can attention guidance close the gap?
